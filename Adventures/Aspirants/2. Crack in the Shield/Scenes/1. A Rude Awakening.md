> You were hoping, given the events of the past few days, for a nice, quiet morning, maybe sleeping in a bit before a nice, warm breakfast. This plan is quickly ruined by angry shouts from the common room.
> 
> You emerge into utter chaos - it seems half the town is here, voice raised shouting over one another.  [[Waterkeeper Tobias]] stands near the bar, weathering it all with a look on his face that tells you he hasn't slept since you last spoke.

As the party steps out of their rooms, they find themselves in a much more crowded common room.  Faces are angry, and among the chaos they can make out a few relatively consistent themes:

* What are you going to do about it?
* What about my home/stuff?
* When is Sir Alaric going to be back?/Is it true the line is down?

Eventually a few faces turn towards the party:

> *"You - you arrived from Stonehollow - when will Sir Alaric get here?  This fool (gestures at Tobias) will see us all dead before the week is out!" -Bram*

The party is thrown into a Negotiation with the crowd - if they try to be silent the crowd will get angry, wonder if maybe the new folks are responsible, and Tobias will gesture desperately at them to defend themselves (verbally). 

* A herd of goats was lost to the Roiling Fog while the heroes slept.
* The Fog apparently came through a breach in the wall unexpectedly.
* Explanations vary, some claim it's poor maintenance, others sabotage. 
	* Regardless, they blame Tobias - either for not requiring stricter maintenance, or for letting someone into the town who would sabotage the wall (they assume it must be an outsider).

## Voices
A few characters are the once most likely to speak up during this negotiation
* [[Waterkeeper Tobias]] - Generally quiet, he recognizes his voice is unlikely to help at this juncture. If prompted he will chime in, but it may backfire.
* [[Elara Vance]] - When the heroes make a successful roll, her's is the voice that chimes in to support them.  She wants to minimize the feeling of risk/harm, and is feeling somewhat guilty.
* [[Silas Croft]] - Seems reserved, but may chime in with skepticism on a poor roll, especially regarding the leadership of the town or the capabilities of the party. He remains civil the whole time, but occasionally makes off-hand comments spurring the others to anger.
* Bram - Ringleader of the argument, wode-elf herdsman (the one who lost the goats)
* Lyra - Dwarf woman, can chime in if the others don't fit - basically just a name in case I need to pull one to fill in a gap.
# Negotiation
The crowd is Suspicious, and so starts with **2 Interest** and **2 Patience**.  Because, in this instance, one hero can simply listen to background chatter to learn about the Motivations or Pitfalls of the crowd, use the follow results instead of the usual table for the first such roll for each (Motivation and Pitfall).

* <=11 - The hero learns no information regarding the NPCs motivations or pitfalls
* 12-16 - The hero learns of one of the NPCs motivations or pitfalls (their choice)
* 17+ - The hero learns of one of the NPCs motivations or pitfalls (their choice) *AND* increases the crowd's Patience by one - they feel like they heroes are listening.

While Bram is clearly taking point for the mob, Silas is steering it.  On a critical success on any roll the party notices that Bram (and much of the rest of the mob) are taking their ques from Silas.  If the party makes a check to see if they can identify the ring-leaders use the following results:

* <=11 - The crowd interprets the looks as accusatory and they lose 1 Patience, but group around Bram, giving his role away
* 12-16 - The party notices that Bram is the face of the mob, and often repeating arguments others have said, just louder.
* 17+ - The party notices that Bram is the face of the mob, as above, but realize many of the whispered complaints he's repeating, are starting from Silas.

**Motivations:**
* Justice - They want whoever is responsible brought to justice. (One exception here being Elara, see below for suggested response on failure to use this motivation)
* Peace - They don't want to be chased from their homes by demons.

**Pitfalls:**
* Higher Authority - They're wrong to blame Tobias, but they currently are, and won't appreciate appeals to "trust" him or the other leaders.
* Discovery - They have no interest in what could be learned from the Roiling Fog, or about their defenses, they just want the Fog out and their defenses to work.

**Outcomes:**
* Interest 5 (Yes, and) - The crowd disperses, Bram apologizes and gives the party a Buzz Balm in appolgy, and Silas and Elara are more open to conversations in the future
* Interest 4 (Yes) - The crowd disperses and Silas and Elara are more open to conversations in the future
* Interest 3 (Yes, but) - The crowd disperses
* Interest 2 (No, but) - The crows disperses, but Silas and Elara are less open to conversations in the future
* Interest 1 (No) - The crowd doesn't disperse; Tobias is forced to call on the guard, and everyone is more distrustful of the party going forward.
* Interest 0 (No, and) - The crowd doesn't disperse, and the guard doesn't answer quickly - a (hopefully) fist-fight ensues between the party and Bram and his friends.  Win or lose, everyone is more distrustful of the party going forward.

## Good/Bad Rolls
**On good rolls**, the following may be useful for inspiration in responses:
* Tobias makes calming gestures, and chimes in to reinforce the party's words: *"Garrick should return in the next few hours; then we'll know more."*
* Elara downplays the damage: *"Yes, it's bad Bram lost his goats, and I'm happy to do my part to help him recover, but it was just goats."*
* Elara downplays the risks: *"I'm sure Garrick has the repairs well in hand; it won't spread any further than it already has."*

On bad rolls, the following may be useful for inspiration in responses:
* Elara gets worried about a bad appeal to the Justice motivation: *"We don't know it was sabotage; no point getting worked up about it until Garrick gets back."*
* Bram gets angry about an appeal to Higher Authority pitfall: *"Him?  You want us to trust him?  He's why we're in this mess!"*
* Silas expresses skepticism in the party's solution: *"Look, I'm sure you mean well, but isn't this a little beyond your pay-grade?"*

# If things go south...
The fight isn't particularly dangerous (3x Human Brawler), and the guards show up after a few rounds.  If the party kills anyone they're likely either arrested or told to leave and not return.  It should be made clear well in advance, however, that using lethal force in a situation like this may see them fail their bid to become Ember Guard.  The rules for Draw Steel mean the party can always decide their damage is non-lethal without any penalties.
