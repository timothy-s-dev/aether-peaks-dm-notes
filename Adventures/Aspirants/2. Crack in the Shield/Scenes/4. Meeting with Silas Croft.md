# Overview
Silas isn’t on the party’s side by default. He knows more than he let on in [[1. A Rude Awakening]], but will only share if he thinks he can turn a profit or secure an advantage. This scene lets sharp players potentially discover:
* Silas thinks it was sabotage.
* He thinks it was an outside organization.
* He thinks someone in town is helping them.

It is worth keeping in mind that the results of the initial Negotiation with the mob may impact Silas' willingness to work with the party here.  If they got Interest 4 or 5, they have a Boon on checks here, and if they got <=2 they have a Bane until they do something to get Silas on their side.

There are several potential opportunities to spend money in this scene, but a single PC can only apply their wealth once with Silas without over-extending and dropping a Wealth level.

The party might encounter him that evening in the Weeping Kettle's taproom (curios to watch them, as it happens), or may go find him at his shop/house. He mostly sells curios - nothing particularly useful to the party as a rule.  The party can afford one such curio each at Wealth 1.  At Wealth 2 they could grab all of those listed below:  

* A "Fog Guage" - A cloudy glass orb on a brass stand, with etched lines labeled: "Clear", "Misty", and "Opaque" - it's utterly useless for detecting the Roiling Fog, but uses internal condensation to indicate normal fog levels... if left upright for at least 15 minutes, in said fog. (think weather rock)
* Self-Righting Tankard - A dented pewter mug that can't easily be knocked over - not so much magical, as just cleverly weighted, though Silas swears it's a minor enchantment.
* Lucky Demon Toe - Clearly a fake carved from wood (not least of all because real demons dissolve into ichor when left outside the Fog too long). Silas claims it "wards off lesser imps," but it absolutely does no such thing.
* Ever-Warm Handkerchief - A square of fabric that always feels pleasantly warm, like it's been in a pocket for hours. Doesn't provide enough heat to meaningfully warm a space or person, but is the one legitimately magical item on offer.
* Miniature Wall Segment - A small, clay model of Dimdale's warded wall - beautifully painted, and surprisingly well crafted, if definitely a tourist item.

Additionally, Silas has, in a fine glass display case, a "Mediator's Charm" - much like Layer 3 below, he won't part with this without a Wealth 3 purchase (or Wealth 2 and drop a level), two Wealth 2 purchases, or a Wealth 2 purchase and a promise of a future favor.  

> Silas sits at a corner table/behind his shop counter, a ledger open before him and a gold coin dancing back and forth across his knuckles. His clothes are a riot of clashing colors. *"Ah, Dimdale's new would-be saviors.  Sit/come in, if you're here to talk business/browse my wares.  If you're here for charity or questions, go speak with Tobias."*

Silas will give out information in layers, depending on how hard and effectively the party pushes him. 

Threats of violence will get the party layer 1, then a bunch of deliberate misinformation; it was two men, outsiders, they wanted revenge on one of the herders, just got the wrong herd, not sure if they'll be back.  Afterwards he goes and digs up that it was Elara and offers to help her, and gets the party ambushed by the Cultists.

## Layer 1
This is information he'll give the party for free, if they press.
* He suspects it was sabotage: *"This wasn’t simple ‘wear and tear.’ Someone wanted that wall weakened. And no, I don’t mean Tobias forgot to have Garrick polish the stones."*
* He suspects multiple parties involved: *"Doing what I do, and -wink- knowing who I know; I learn things. Let's just say I have reason to believe there's more than one party involved."*

## Layer 2
For this information the party will need to put up a small bribe. A character with 2 wealth can afford a sufficient bribe. A character with 1 wealth can make the bribe, but goes down to 0 wealth by doing so.  Alternatively, the party may make a competent argument that business would be better if this whole thing was cleared up.
* There were outsiders involved: *"Let's just say I overheard some... guests, at the Weeping Kettle.  Not locals.  Quiet voices asking the wrong kinds of questions about the wards, about 'testing' them, and the Fog."*
* If the party presses, or asks about who those questions were directed at, he reveals there was a local involves as well: *"Was a local they were asking. Not sure who, though {pause} they seemed knowledgeable."*
	* The pause, if the party checks/rolls, indicates he was about to use a pronoun and give away a gender, but caught himself.

## Layer 3
For this information the party would need to put up a very large bribe.  Same as above, but need 3 wealth to make it without consequence, or 2 at the cost of dropping a Wealth level.  Alternatively, they could have critically succeeded on their previous check, or could offer Silas a hefty future favor (personal Ember Guard escort for a future delivery, or something delivered unopened and no-questions asked by themselves maybe, once they earn their emblems.)  He has them sign something if they go the future favor route, figuring he can bring it up with the Ember Guard to tarnish their reputations if they try to renege on their promise.
* The local was a human-sized woman. *"Was a woman, the local that is, and probably about five-and-a-half foot or so.  Couldn't place the voice, and I didn't go craning my neck around to look under her hood - like my eyes just where they are, as it happens."*  
* The local was interested in information, not coin. *"She met them late though, and they didn't pay her, least not in coin or up front - I know the sound well enough to notice its absence. She seemed interested enough in their "research" though, the effects the Fog has and whatnot."*
