> Dimdale lies in in a valley, shielded on three sides by steep cliff-walls, and on the last by a huge stone barrier wall.  A dim glow is visible in the evening light even from this distance coming off the wall.
> 
> In that valley lies a town different from most you're familiar with; instead of clinging to cliff walls or built into cliff-side caves it's built flat, spreading wildly across the open ground.
> 
> Where most cities rely on tall, magical hydroponic spires for food, here large fields spread over the open ground sowed with whatever tough vegetables the farmers can get to grow in the high altitudes.
> 
> As you approach the gates the guards part, not for you, but for a tall older man, lean and weathered, who approached from behind them.

The man who greets the party is Waterkeeper Tobias.
* A tall man in his fifties, lean and weathered, with pale eyes and a thin braid of silver hair down one shoulder.
* Wears dark blue robes belted with copper rings — a sign of his office. His hands are stained with chalk from marking ration ledgers and ward maps.
* Speaks with quiet authority and deliberate calm — like someone used to coaxing people through bad news without spreading panic.

> Tobias' face seems eager as he approaches; he looks over the party carefully then deflates slightly. He recovers quickly though, and says: "I'm sorry, you are, of course, most welcome in Dimdale, but if I'm to be honest I was hoping to see Sir Alaric when I saw figures descending the path. I'm Waterkeeper Tobias, at your service."

* If the party doesn't offer their story, he'll prompt them for it.  
	* If they don't share but have Vinn with them, she'll share the bit she knows
* He'll guide them to the local inn and tavern, the Weeping Kettle.
* If he becomes aware of what they've been through he offers to cover their food and first night at the inn.
* If he becomes aware of the line being broken he seems deeply bothered by it.
	* If pressed, he will admit they're having difficulty with the barrier wall, and he was hoping Sir Alaric was going to return soon and be able to help.
	* He prefers not to elaborate - it's not the party's problem, and they've been through enough. If they're interested in the morning he's confident someone will fill them in on the details.
* Once they're settled at the inn and he's heard their story or been firmly rebuffed he'll leave them to it.  Most of the townspeople leave them alone - the mood is noticeably quiet for a tavern in the evening - people seem worried.
* If they push they can find out there's a rumor someone sabotaged the barrier wall, and there is worry the fog will break through soon.
