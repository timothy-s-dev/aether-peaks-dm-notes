The party has a few moments to help each other up, dust themselves off, and look around before the Fog boils up over the ridge.

> For a few heartbeats, there’s only the wind. It snakes over the ridge, tugging at cloaks and scattering loose stones down the slope. The others are stirring now — coughing, swearing, checking for wounds. 
> 
> Then the world goes quiet. Even the wind seems to hold its breath. The Fog reaches the ridge like a living thing, tendrils rolling over the stone — pale gray at first, then bruised green beneath, pulsing faintly from within. 
> 
> A low chattering sound rises out of it, thin and insectile, quickly swelling into a hundred overlapping whispers. Shapes burst from the haze — small, skittering, too fast to count. Their eyes gleam a sickly emerald as they scuttle on clawed limbs, half-rat, half-insect, bile dripping from their maws. 
> 
> The Fog surges again, climbing the hill towards you nearly as fast as the charging demons. Draw Steel!

The party is attacked by a horde of demons:

For 3 PCs: 8 Pitlings, 12 Ensnarers, 1 Muceron
For 4 PCs: add 4 Pitlings, and 4 Ensnarers
For 5 PCs: Add 4 Pitlings and 1 Muceron

The battlemap either needs to be quite long, or else have obstacles I can move along it to simulate that length. The fight will be a running one, with the fog rising 4 squares towards them per round. It starts 8 squares away, -1 for each tick on the clock. The fog can be driven back with wind or fire abilities; either can clear it in a 2-3 square burst around the impact. If a hero is caught within the fog at the start of their turn they take 3 corruption damage. The fog will only advance for 4 rounds, after which it reaches “High Tide” and stops, though the demons make no such concession.

## A Brief Moment of Respite

Once the party emerges victorious they have a moment to recover and discuss.

* The corpses of the demons dissolve into smears of dark ichor that sizzles against the rock.  Touching it burns (1 corruption damage)
* The fog has stopped, having reached its "high tide" mark.
* A couple things seem strange:
	* The demons don't usually come to the very edge of the fog, much less leave it
	* Cable cars don't generally fail - you can count on one and the stories of failures like that in the last hundred years across the Empire.
* They notice they've lost most of their gear, basically anything that wasn't attached to them before the crash. 
* Resting would be ill-advised; while the Roiling Fog has stopped climbing, there are other dangers in the mountain that won't constrain themselves to its concealment.

When they're ready to move on, they climb a short ways before discovering the cable draped against the mountain. They can use it to climb, or just as a point of reference to follow back to the pass.
